# Progression

## Status

The progression structure is **CURRENT DIRECTION**. The major axes are well established, but exact numbers, tier counts, area count, and late-game systems are intentionally not final.

## Progression promise

The player should feel smarter in three different ways at once:

1. **Numerically:** IQ rises.
2. **Functionally:** harder things become comprehensible and better tools become usable.
3. **Visually:** the player's plot, equipment, accessible world, and targets become dramatically more advanced.

If one of those three is missing, progression will feel flatter than intended.

## Primary progression axes

### IQ — capability

IQ is the central long-term stat.

Higher IQ should let the player:
- comprehend harder items
- enter later areas
- use advanced study/research technology
- eventually understand better traversal tools/shortcuts

IQ should generally accumulate rather than being spent like cash.

### Money — infrastructure

Money is the main spendable resource.

Money should primarily buy:
- Study Station upgrades
- Research Center construction/upgrades
- future authored plot structures
- possibly transportation/support equipment where appropriate

Money gives the player short- and medium-term purchase goals while IQ represents lasting capability.

### Visualized-item progression — content quality

The player should always want access to a better thing to visualize/train with.

Better objects may be better because they are:
- more mentally complex
- physically larger
- stranger or less familiar
- more advanced technologically
- more valuable
- modified/mutated later if that system proves worthwhile

Do not assume rarity/mutations are required. The basic difficulty ladder should work without them first.

### Study Station — training efficiency

The Study Station is the primary repeatable plot upgrade path.

Its visual and functional progression should be extremely obvious:

```text
book / simple desk
→ stronger study setup
→ chess / logic setup
→ computer
→ advanced workstation
→ research / cognition technology
→ extreme late-game mental technology
```

Exact tiers are not locked.

### Plot — physical progression

The plot is a controlled display of player advancement.

Upgrades appear in predetermined authored locations rather than through free placement.

Current plot progression layers:
1. Study Station
2. Research Center build location
3. reserved item/display or future-system space
4. room for later authored additions if they solve a real gameplay need

### Areas — breadth and aspiration

Areas contain different object pools and visual themes.

New areas should be framed as things the player has become able to comprehend/access, not merely generic numbered worlds.

The starting area is the floating Apple Orchard. Later area themes are intentionally not locked yet.

### Movement — reduced friction

As areas become larger or progression becomes more repetitive, traversal should improve.

Thematic directions include:
- bicycle / simple gear
- hoverboard or advanced mobility
- shortcuts that become understandable at higher IQ
- teleportation / spatial comprehension later

Movement should not become faster solely because the IQ number increased with no in-world explanation.

## Unlock philosophy

Use **IQ** when the fantasy is:

> My mind is not ready to understand this yet.

Use **money** when the fantasy is:

> I can afford/build a better tool.

Use **IQ + money** when the fantasy is:

> I understand the technology, but I still need the resources to construct it.

Avoid inventing a third currency when IQ or money already explains the unlock.

## Progression cadence

### First 5 minutes

Goals:
- first apple visualized
- first IQ gain
- first Study Station interaction
- first visible purchase/upgrade
- player sees at least one harder future target
- player sees the next-area gate

### First 15–30 minutes

Desired experience:
- several meaningful IQ thresholds crossed
- multiple Study Station visual changes
- Research Center becomes attainable or clearly aspirational
- player graduates from the easiest orchard content toward the next content tier
- Area 2 becomes the major near-term goal

Exact pacing must be playtested rather than designed only on paper.

### Around 1 hour

Desired feeling:
- starting setup looks noticeably primitive compared with the current setup
- player has experienced at least one major environmental/content change
- better items noticeably accelerate progression
- a new movement convenience or world shortcut may begin to matter

### Long-term

The game should escalate in spectacle rather than merely adding zeros.

Illustrative escalation only:

```text
apple / simple fruit
→ books / games / tools / ordinary objects
→ machines / vehicles
→ large structures / complex systems
→ advanced technology
→ massive/cosmic things
→ impossible objects
→ abstract/conceptual targets
```

The exact sequence is not locked. The important property is that each major stage feels more intellectually absurd than the previous one.

## Social progression

Another player should be able to infer advancement by looking at:
- Study Station tier
- Research Center state
- later plot technology
- advanced item displays or effects if used
- which areas/objects the player can access

Do not rely only on floating numbers above players' heads to communicate progression.

## Power curve principles

- early upgrades should be frequent and easy to understand
- later upgrades can take longer if they visibly change the game
- better items and better Study Station tiers should multiply each other's value enough to make both progression paths matter
- avoid exponential inflation so extreme that older content becomes meaningless instantly unless that is a deliberate reset point
- keep formulas data-driven and tunable
